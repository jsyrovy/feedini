Lighthouse Keeper Retires After Forty Years

The keeper of the Gullrock lighthouse climbed its one hundred and twelve steps for the last time on Friday. For forty years he wound the clockwork, cleaned the lens and wrote the weather into a logbook that now fills a whole shelf of the harbor museum.

The light has run on its own since an automatic lamp was installed in the nineties, but the town kept the post because the keeper also ran the small rescue boat. A volunteer crew takes over the boat, and the logbooks will be scanned by school pupils over the winter.

Asked what he will miss, the keeper named the storms in November, when the whole tower hums, and the quiet mornings in May, when the seals come back to the rocks below the gallery.
